Sacred Sandalwood

Sandalwood, mainly from the Santalum album tree, is one of the oldest and most loved aromatic materials in the world. Its scent is creamy, warm and woody, and it has been a mainstay of ritual and perfumery for thousands of years.

The essence of scarcity

Sandalwood scent lingers for a very long time, but the tree is slow. A sandalwood tree must reach maturity, often 15 to 30 years, before the heartwood holds enough oil to be worth harvesting. Over-harvesting in the wild means Santalum album is now listed as vulnerable on the IUCN Red List.

Historical significance

From the ancient temples of India to the imperial courts of China, sandalwood has long stood for the sacred.

  • Spiritual: in many Eastern traditions its smoke is burned in meditation and prayer to quiet the mind and mark a space as set apart.
  • Craft: the fine, even grain carves well, and sandalwood has been made into beads, fans and small boxes for centuries.
  • Perfumery: it is a classic “fixative”, binding other scents and helping them last.

Other sandalwoods

Australian sandalwood (Santalum spicatum) has also gained ground in recent years, with a slightly different, greener scent.
